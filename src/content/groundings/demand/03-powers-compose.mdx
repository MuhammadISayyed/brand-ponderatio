Suppose Parts 1 and 2 are granted. Demand is a power, and not a disguised
conditional. Economics still has to add it up — individual demand into market
demand, market demand into aggregate demand — and the question is what
operation that addition is.

The default answer is that it is addition. Market demand at £4 is the sum of
the quantities each buyer would take at £4. This is so nearly universal that
it reads as arithmetic rather than as a claim, and it is a claim: it says that
demands combine the way quantities combine, without interacting.

## What powers do instead

<Cite source="martin-mutual">Martin</Cite> argued that powers manifest
*mutually*. A disposition does not act on a passive world; it meets other
dispositions, and what appears is a joint manifestation for which neither
partner is sufficient and which is not the sum of two separate effects. The
salt's solubility and the water's dissolving power are not two causes adding
up. They are reciprocal partners producing one outcome — and neither is
exercised at all without the other.

<Cite source="mumford-anjum">Mumford and Anjum</Cite> give this a geometry.
Powers are modelled as vectors in a quality space, composing into a resultant
rather than accumulating. Two things follow that matter here. Composition is
not commutative with context: the same two powers in a different arrangement
give a different resultant. And a resultant can be *zero* while both component
powers are fully present and fully operative — mutual cancellation, which is
invisible in the outcome and entirely real in the causal
structure.<Sidenote>This is the vector model's most useful consequence for
economics: it makes "nothing happened" compatible with "two strong causes
operated", which no regularity-based account can express.</Sidenote>

<Cite source="molnar">Molnar</Cite> adds the point that powers are *directed*
— they are for something, at something — and directedness does not add. Two
forces pointing at different manifestations do not produce a bigger version of
either.

## Why aggregation was always going to be strange

Read market demand as a sum of independent quantities and every interaction
effect becomes an anomaly to be patched: network effects, bandwagons,
conspicuous consumption, herding, the way a shortage produces buying that the
shortage itself created. Each gets its own correction term, and the corrections
have nothing in common but their embarrassment.

Read market demand as a composition of powers and the same phenomena are
predictions. Of course a buyer's readiness is partly directed at other
buyers' readiness — that is what it is to want a thing that is scarce, or
fashionable, or worth having because others have it. Of course the resultant
is not the sum, since the components were never independent. Of course two
strong and opposed movements can leave the ledger flat.

The mathematics of demand aggregation has known for a long time that
individual rationality does not aggregate into anything well-behaved. The
usual response is to restrict the conditions until it does. The dispositional
reading suggests the restriction was hiding the finding: demands were never
the kind of thing that sums, and a model that sums them is not approximating
the market, it is describing a different one.
