The essay column had to hold something close to 66 characters a line. The
usual way to get there is the alphabet-advance method: set the lowercase
alphabet in the face and at the size you intend to use, measure how wide it
runs, and multiply. The multiplier depends on whose table you are reading —
1.87 for a 66-character measure is the common one, and "two and a half
alphabets" is the same rule stated loosely.

Done properly for Source Serif 4 at 20px, that method gives 58.4 characters
for a 640px column. Which would mean 640px is too narrow by some margin, and
the column should open up to around 730px.

It isn't, and it shouldn't.

## What the estimate leaves out

The alphabet advance averages the 26 lowercase letters and nothing else. Real
text is roughly one space in every five or six characters, and the space
glyph is narrower than the mean lowercase advance — in this face,
substantially so. Averaging only the letters therefore overstates the width of
an average character, and a method that overstates character width
understates how many characters fit.

The error runs one way. Every estimate produced this way is low, which is why
following it lands you with a column wider than the one you wanted.

## What I did instead

Set real prose in the column and counted the rendered line boxes rather than
predicting them:

- ten paragraphs of running text, not lorem ipsum — the space frequency is
  the whole point, and dummy Latin has the wrong one
- counted characters per line from the actual line breaks
- discarded the ragged last line of each paragraph, which is short for
  reasons that have nothing to do with the measure

That gives **64.4 characters** at 640px. Inside the target, and six characters
off what the estimate promised.

## The number is not portable

64.4 is a fact about Source Serif 4 at 20px and nothing else. It is not a
fact about `--grid-main-col`, and it does not survive the font swap that
`tokens.css` is built to accommodate: Tiempos Text sets tighter, so the same
640px will hold more.

So `--grid-main-col` carries the measurement and the conditions in a comment,
and this note is `working` rather than `revised` because the second
measurement — the one against the paid faces — has not been taken. When it
is, the token changes and this note gets superseded rather than edited.
