title: Accounts of the Market

standfirst: >
  Marketing talks about markets as though they were settled territory — as
  though advertising and a snapshot of who bought what were enough to explain
  how a market comes to be, why demand takes the shapes it does, and why some
  markets die while others are reborn. Those questions are live and well
  argued in other disciplines, and largely unread in this one. This inquiry
  works through the accounts of the market that causality, complex systems and
  innovation research have already built, and asks what they oblige a marketer
  to believe.

The marketer's job is that of a juggler. You are tasked with exchanging value
back and forth between your company and its customers, juggling supply, demand,
competition and market conditions. And moving forward.

Each of the aforementioned points requires a unique skillset to handle it. Poor
marketers look like they really need help. Like jugglers, we need to handle many
things at once, unlike them, we do not understand how these things move, their
underlying principles or their governing dynamics.

In this we are closer to Harry Potter chasing the golden snitch than we are to a
juggler. The bigger problem is that we believe that our golden snitches are just
regular balls governed by the well-known laws of gravity. Golden snitches of the
market keep hitting from all sides, but we keep our hands raised, expecting the
balls to fall gracefully at our palms.

Marketing's current perception of market behavior and dynamics is reductive to
the point of being downright harmful. Our discussions revolve around advertising
and whether "scientific" descriptions of markets at a single point in time are
capable of resolving every marketing challenge once and for all.

There are much more fundamental questions whose answers contribute to the
marketer's worldview much more than the ones discussed and rediscussed right
now...

How do markets come to be?

Is demand met or created?

Is demand always about pain points? If not, how to make sense of different
demand shapes?

Why do some markets age and die while others are reborn and amplified?

If advertising is a weak power, do we have something strong at hand to make
customers pick our products and services over the competition?

And while we are at it, what causes our need to buy something?

As far as I know, answers to these questions do exist from different
disciplines. They are actively critiqued, evaluated and reintroduced to reality
for evaluation. What is absent is our interest, as marketers, in engaging with
them. We are busy fighting for the advertising budget, we're busy watching our
back.

But like anything important, you gotta make time for it. In this inquiry we will
explore different accounts of the market and how it works based on studies of
causality, complex systems and innovation between other things. This should
allow for a holistic view of how markets behave and change over time, a view
that we desperately need as marketers to be taken seriously, but most
importantly to take ourselves seriously.
