The cheapest objection to Part 1 is that it has renamed something. Dispositional
talk, on a long and respectable view, is shorthand: to call a thing soluble is
to say it would dissolve if immersed, and the disposition is a way of speaking
about the conditional rather than a further fact underneath it.
<Cite source="ryle">Ryle</Cite> put dispositional statements in the category of
inference tickets — licences to predict, not descriptions of hidden states.

If that view is right, this inquiry is over. A demand schedule already *is* a
set of conditionals, so restating it as a disposition adds a word and no
content.

The view is not right, and the reason is worth having precisely, because the
failure recurs in the economic case with the labels changed.

## The simple conditional analysis

Write it as the tradition does:

> x is disposed to M when C  ≡  if C were the case, x would M.

Fragile: would break if struck. Soluble: would dissolve if immersed. Demanded
at £4: would be bought if offered at £4. The analysis is clean, and it fails
in two directions.

<Cite source="martin">Martin</Cite> supplied the first. Imagine a live wire —
disposed to conduct when touched by a conductor — attached to a device that
detects the touch and removes the disposition microseconds before current
could flow. The wire is live. The conditional is false: were it touched, it
would not conduct. A **fink** is anything that removes the disposition in the
very circumstances that would have manifested it. Martin's second case runs the
other way: a dead wire wired to a device that makes it live the instant it is
touched. The conditional is true and the wire is dead.

<Cite source="johnston">Johnston</Cite> supplied the second, which needs no
machinery. Pack a fragile glass in styrofoam. It is still fragile — nothing
about the glass changed — but it would not break if struck. A **mask** leaves
the disposition intact and blocks the manifestation. Masks are not exotic;
they are the ordinary condition of things in the world, which is why crockery
survives being posted.

<Cite source="lewis">Lewis</Cite> accepted the finks and reformed the analysis
to quantify over the *causal basis* of the disposition, requiring it to be
retained long enough to do its work. The repair handles finks. It does not
handle masks without further epicycles, and — more to the present point — it
concedes the substantive claim. The reformed analysis works by referring to an
intrinsic property that grounds the disposition. That property is not a
conditional. The conditional has become a symptom of the disposition rather
than an analysis of it.

## The same failure, in a market

Now run all three against demand, and notice that the examples arrive
unbidden.

A buyer wants the good and would buy at £4. Her card is declined; the payment
system is down; she has the money on Tuesday and the offer expires Monday. Her
demand is **masked**. Nothing about her wanting has changed and no transaction
occurs.<Sidenote>Note that revealed preference cannot tell this case apart
from a genuine absence of demand, because both produce the same record: no
purchase at £4. The two are distinguished by facts about her, which is exactly
the sort of fact the method was designed to avoid appealing to.</Sidenote>

A buyer would buy at £4, and the seller's dynamic pricing engine observes her
interest and raises the price the moment she looks. Her demand is **finked** —
the very conditions that would manifest it destroy it. The engine is
Martin's electro-fink with a revenue target.

And the reverse case is a marketing department: a buyer with no antecedent
demand at £4, who acquires one when the campaign reaches her, so that the
conditional is true and there was nothing there before it was made true. The
schedule cannot distinguish demand discovered from demand manufactured, since
both show up as a purchase at £4.

None of this is a complaint about measurement error. It is the observation
that the conditional and the disposition come apart systematically, in both
directions, for structural reasons — and that when they come apart, our
judgement follows the disposition and not the conditional. We say she wanted
it and could not buy it. That sentence is unintelligible if demand *is* the
conditional, and obvious if demand is a power that the conditional usually,
but not always, reveals.
