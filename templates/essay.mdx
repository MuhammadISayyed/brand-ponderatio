Open on the claim, not on the throat-clearing. The first paragraph should be
the argument in miniature — a reader who stops here should still have been told
something.

## Sections use two hashes

Never `#` — the page title is already the only `<h1>`. `###` exists if a
section genuinely needs a subdivision.

A sidenote attaches to the end of the sentence it qualifies, with no space
before the tag.<Sidenote>This is the qualification. Numbered automatically,
restarting at 1 on every page; the marker and the note link to each other. On a
phone it drops inline.</Sidenote> Sidenotes carry the asides that would
otherwise bloat a paragraph.

Citations name the author in the prose and connect that name to the entry at
the foot — there is no superscript number. <Cite source="alexander">Alexander</Cite>
made this point sixty years ago.

> A blockquote sets as a pull quote. Use it for the passage you are about to
> take apart, not for emphasis.

## Close on the test

End with what would have to be true for the argument to be wrong, or with the
test a reader can apply themselves. Not a summary — they have just read it.
